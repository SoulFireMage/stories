"Dammit, he's done it again. Frikkin tyre mark on the wall, bloody great puddle on the floor. I'm gonna rub his hood in it, come here git, where are you?" I said, rushing outside where I could hear his high pitched gear change, squeal of tyres as Rascal raced off to his Dad.

I reach the garage, I heard him trying to be quiet as he snuck away trying to hide.
" Come out, come out, I wanna know where you learnt that trick from."...

***
 "Could ya stop marking ya territory you four wheel terror...!"

 Oh no, not the neighbour's yard, again. Oh yes, here he comes. 

I watch helpless between wanting to giggle and wanting to yell as once again Rascal is racing racing the corner, sliding and bouncing as he drifts on two wheels, and my formidable neighbour flies after him, a lumbering bombardier of a woman, broom in hand, swiping at his miniature jacked up frame.

He heads for the safety of our kitchen, I'm still trying not to giggle out loud. The lumbering lady pulls to a halt, broom stops inches from my nose. 

"Are you gonna control that little monster or am I gonna catch me a freak and put him on Ebay." She yelled, her whiskey stinking breath searing my skin. 

She's such a fucking shape bigot. Despite all the pies, the help and time we've given to try and get to know Mrs Lomax, she just cannot accept our kids as kids. 

The rest of the neighbours, they're great. They took to the cubs the first time they met them. Tbh I can't blame Rascal for choosing his target, though I can fault his sense of strategy and timing. And I do wish they'd stop figuring out how to get out of the garden. I mean, they're young trucks NOT monkeys, how the hell are they doing it?

Scout's no better, just subtler. The incidence of drive-by knock-door-run went up after they first met the battling Bertha Lomax.

We were doing the neighbour thing, introducing ourselves properly. Most neighbours came to our housewarming and quickly learnt that cats can't outrun infant trucks. However, several neighbours were more reserved, so we went to see them.  When she met the cubs, she wrinkled her nose, looked down at them and said, "What the hell are those?". This was immediately followed by disdainful remarks about freaks of nature, demonspawn and other complimentary comments.

Mixed mech families were very rare but completely protected by law of course. Not that they'd been much choice. However, not everyone accepted them completely.

"Mrs Lomax, we're very sorry if he's ruined something of yours again, of course we'll sort it out. However, I now have a problem with you." I said, speaking quietly, so quietly that Bertha had to lean in to hear me. "If you ever, ever touch one of our children again, you'll find out exactly just how hard I can swing your fucking broom. You'll be sweeping your yard with your tongue. And THEN I'll call Child Protection Services on you."

I stepped forwards, slowly, deliberate and stared right into her stinking face. "Do I make myself clear? For the last time, they are not freaks, they're our children. And if one them damages your property you come to ME. I'll handle them and your damages. So turn your ass around and get off my property."

She went through every colour of the rainbow as I spoke...then white as a ghost. Child Protection was serious business now. Ever since the mechs started appearing and laws got mysteriously enhanced, all kinds of problems that were ignored, once, became priority number one. Bigotry and abuse were stamped on hard and fast.

She left quietly. I turned, my impulse to laugh gone now. "Rascal, come here."

 His gears whined as he rolled guiltily across the kitchen floor. "What were you doing?"

His mirrors drooped, he lowered his nose, a proper pathetic picture. "Oh no you don't, you think you're gonna get away with it by pulling that?"

Trouble was, he knew I couldn't be mad at him for long. They both know it.

I sat down cross legged in front of him, picked him up and held him close. "Can you please, please, please leave that old battleaxe alone? She's never gonna be a friend to you guys and, let's face it, peeing in her yard ain't gonna change her any. 'Cept mebbe give her a heart attack I guess."

He revved at me, his wheels spun a little. He's not quite old enough to speak yet, but he does well enough without words. I heard his sister trying to sneak up behind me, her gears whine just as much as his, and those knobbly tyres, yeah they ain't ninja gear. Still I pretend I don't notice, just to see what she tries this time. Usually it's a flying leap onto my back or into my arms if my position is right. She's quite an acrobat, for a truck.

 "Umph....ow" It was the back this time, she got air from one of the many toy ramps left around the place. "Don't you tangle my hair, you!" 

I forgot to put it up, though I swear I'll be bloody bald before these two get too big to do this. It's like they've no idea that hair tangles in wheels and hair is attached to my head.

Our house is a bit special, it has to be, one of us weighs several tons and our children will grow to the same weight. So when my husband pulls into the drive, he ends up in our living room, his 38" tyres next to my own seat. 

"Hi darling" I yelled through when his rumble died down a bit. "Would you like some cubs? At least one has become rather attached to my hair, again." Scrambling to my feet, I hold Rascal in one hand, grab Scout in the other just so she don't yank my hair out by the root. 

"You look contented right there." He snarks back, "I think I'll just watch the racing." He flips the telly on with his built in remote and attempted to ignore his offspring. Doesn't last - I plant Rascal on his bonnet, freeing up my other hand to tackle the torture puzzle that is the sum of my hair and Scout. I finally get her free with only minimal loss of blond tresses. 

I dump her through her Dad's open window. "Present for you!" 

I feel better already - anger forgotten, hubby's home and, well, there's nothing more I love - other than him - than playing with my cubs. .
